# `arm_events.shm/1` — what happens to the arms

**Contract version:** 1 (`schema_version` 1). **Transport:** iceoryx2
`=0.9.3`. **Byte layout:** `1.layout.json` beside this file, which is
normative for every offset, size, code and service setting; the tables below
mirror it and a test fails when they drift apart. **Byte layout SHA-256:**
`2377745c6660434a2b805afb5feb21b7299a38cbfa03928b3e53a1d87142d619`. **Rust definitions:** crate `d1fw-arm-shm-contract`. **Daemon
side:** `d1-firmwared`'s arm publisher (`[arm] shm_publish`).

One fixed-size event per thing that happens to the arms, as it happens:
changes the arm controller reports (its state, its error code, its
frame-miss band), changes of the daemon's own state (the soft-kill latch,
the lease, the brakes, the trajectory lane), and what the daemon does to a
command on any lane (a motion-guard refusal, approach slowing, an emergency
stop, a stop of the `arm_command.shm/1` stream). Every kind is defined
below; a reader that needs a state rather than its changes reads
`arm_status.shm/1`.

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD and MAY are to be read as in RFC 2119.

## 1. Transport and root

As `arm_feedback.shm/1` §1: iceoryx2 `0.9.3`, the end effectors' root and
prefix, a reader running as the daemon's account.

## 2. Services

The base is `omakase/arm` (the daemon's default).

| service | pattern | payload | writer | reader | history | buffer | safe overflow | max writers | max readers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `<base>/events` | pub/sub | `Event` | daemon | reader | 64 | 256 | true | 1 | 8 |

History 64: a reader that starts gets the last 64 events on the next one
published. A reader that falls behind by more than 256 loses the oldest and
counts them from the gaps in `seq`.

## 3. Messages

The conventions are those of `arm_feedback.shm/1` §3. The type name carries
this contract's major (`omakase_arm_events_v1_*`).

### 3.1 Layouts

#### `Header` — `omakase_ee_v1_Header`, 32 bytes, align 8

| offset | size | field | type |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | 4 | `schema_version` | `u32` |
| 4 | 4 | `reserved` | `u32` |
| 8 | 8 | `instance_id` | `u64` |
| 16 | 8 | `seq` | `u64` |
| 24 | 8 | `mono_ns` | `u64` |

#### `Event` — `omakase_arm_events_v1_Event`, 208 bytes, align 8

| offset | size | field | type |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | 32 | `header` | `Header` |
| 32 | 4 | `kind` | `u32` |
| 36 | 4 | `sides` | `u32` |
| 40 | 4 | `code` | `i32` |
| 44 | 4 | `joint` | `i32` |
| 48 | 32 | `values` | `f64[4]` |
| 80 | 128 | `text` | `u8[128]` |

### 3.2 Codes

| name | value | meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `SIDE_MASK_A` | 1 | `Event.sides` bit: arm A |
| `SIDE_MASK_B` | 2 | `Event.sides` bit: arm B |
| `EVENT_CONTROLLER_STATE` | 1 | one arm's controller state (`cur_state`) changed. `code`: the new state; `values[0]`: the previous one |
| `EVENT_CONTROLLER_ERROR` | 2 | one arm's controller error code changed. `code`: the new code; `values[0]`: the previous one |
| `EVENT_FRAME_MISS_HIGH` | 3 | one arm's `frame_miss_count` reached 20, the vendor's unhealthy band. `code`: the count |
| `EVENT_FRAME_MISS_NORMAL` | 4 | it fell back below 20. `code`: the count |
| `EVENT_SOFT_KILL_LATCHED` | 5 | the arm soft-kill latch rose |
| `EVENT_SOFT_KILL_RELEASED` | 6 | the arm soft-kill latch was released |
| `EVENT_LEASE_CHANGED` | 7 | the arm lease changed hands, was granted, expired or was released. `code`: the holder's class rank (`0` for none); `values[0]`: its epoch; `text`: its holder (empty for none) |
| `EVENT_BRAKES_RELEASED` | 8 | the daemon released one arm's brakes for hand guiding |
| `EVENT_BRAKES_ENGAGED` | 9 | the daemon engaged them again |
| `EVENT_GUARD_REFUSED` | 10 | the motion guard refused a joint move, on any lane. `sides`: the arms it addressed; `text`: the verb and the refusal |
| `EVENT_APPROACH_SLOWED` | 11 | the approach limiter wrote a streamed both-arm step short of its target (at most one event per second of a slowed stream). `values[0]`, `values[1]`: arm A's and arm B's step scale; `sides`, `joint`: the arm and joint that decided it, when one did |
| `EVENT_STREAM_STOPPED` | 12 | the `arm_command.shm/1` stream stopped: a command was refused after an accepted one. `code`: the refusal's `RESULT_*`; `text`: the refusal |
| `EVENT_STREAM_RESUMED` | 13 | the `arm_command.shm/1` stream resumed: a command was accepted after a refused one |
| `EVENT_EMERGENCY_STOP` | 14 | one arm was emergency-stopped (`POST /v1/arm/{side}/estop` or its WebSocket method) |
| `EVENT_TRAJECTORY_STARTED` | 15 | a trajectory job started. `values[0]`: its identifier |
| `EVENT_TRAJECTORY_ENDED` | 16 | a trajectory job ended. `code`: its `arm_status.shm/1` `TRAJECTORY_*` phase; `values[0]`: its identifier |

`joint` is `-1` unless the kind names a joint. Unused `values` are zero.

The controller's protective stop is an `EVENT_CONTROLLER_STATE` into its
error state (`cur_state` 100) and an `EVENT_CONTROLLER_ERROR` with its code.

## 4. Rules a reader MUST follow

1. Open the service with the settings of §2 as a subscriber, and never
   publish on it.
2. Check `schema_version`; a change of the header `instance_id` is a
   restarted daemon, whose events start again from `seq` 1.
3. Count gaps in `seq` as lost events.
4. Ignore a `kind` it does not know.

## 5. What the daemon does

- Controller state, error and frame-miss events are raised on the arm
  backend's receive path, from consecutive feedback records.
- Latch, lease, brake and trajectory events are raised when the status is
  built (every 100 ms, and at once for the latch), from the change since the
  previous status.
- Guard refusals, approach slowing and emergency stops are raised by the
  daemon's facade, whichever frontend (REST, WebSocket, ROS 2, iceoryx2,
  `arm_command.shm/1`) sent the command.
- Stream events are raised by the `arm_command.shm/1` path, once per change
  between accepted and refused: a refused command after an accepted one
  stops the stream, and the first accepted one after resumes it. A stop
  locks nothing: the arms hold the last accepted target, and the next
  command that passes every check is written.

## 6. Versioning

- Any change to a layout, a code or a service setting is a new major
  version: `arm_events.shm/2`, type name `omakase_arm_events_v2_Event`, a
  `2.layout.json` sibling, and `schema_version` 2. A new event kind is such
  a change.
- Version 1 is frozen. The SHA-256 of `1.layout.json` is
  `2377745c6660434a2b805afb5feb21b7299a38cbfa03928b3e53a1d87142d619`; the crate pins it
  (`layout::EVENTS_LAYOUT_SHA256`), and the daemon pins it again in its own
  tests.

## 7. Conformance checklist for a reader

1. Runs as the daemon's account; builds its node with the root and prefix
   of `arm_feedback.shm/1` §1; iceoryx2 0.9.3.
2. Opens `<base>/events` with the settings of §2 as a subscriber.
3. Defines `Event` with exactly the layout of `1.layout.json` and the type
   name it lists, and checks the file's SHA-256 in a test.
4. Follows the rules of §4.

## 8. A worked example

A ROS 2 bridge republishes each event as a diagnostic message:
`EVENT_GUARD_REFUSED` with its `text` at WARN, `EVENT_CONTROLLER_STATE` into
`100` (the protective stop) at ERROR with the arm from `sides`, and
`EVENT_LEASE_CHANGED` with the new holder from `text`.
