# `arm_command.shm/1` — joint commands at the controller's rate, through the lease and the guard

**Contract version:** 1 (`schema_version` 1). **Transport:** iceoryx2
`=0.9.3`. **Byte layout:** `1.layout.json` beside this file, which is
normative for every offset, size, code and service setting; the tables below
mirror it and a test fails when they drift apart. **Byte layout SHA-256:**
`987222e48bb079782e01a5d541b0bf19017a67fdf53dbacbc4172d278493c4be`. **Rust definitions:** crate `d1fw-arm-shm-contract` (modules
`wire`, `model`, `ports`); a measuring client is `d1-arm-loopbench`.
**Daemon side:** `d1-firmwared`'s arm command thread (`[arm] shm_command`,
`shm_max_command_hz`).

A client — a ROS 2 controller, a teleoperation station, a policy runner —
streams joint targets for the D1's arms to the daemon (`d1-firmwared`) over
shared memory, up to one per arm controller cycle (1 kHz), instead of one
HTTP request per target. The daemon writes each to the arm controller
through the same checks every other way of commanding the arms passes: the
arm lease, the joint limits, the soft-kill latch, the arms' mode, the
approach limiter and the motion guard. Nothing here bypasses them; what this
contract removes is the HTTP/JSON transport around them.

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD and MAY are to be read as in RFC 2119.

## 1. Transport and root

As `arm_feedback.shm/1` §1: iceoryx2 `0.9.3`, the end effectors' root and
prefix, a client running as the daemon's account.

## 2. Services

The base is `omakase/arm` (the daemon's default). The `command` name is used
twice: once as a publish-subscribe service and once as an event service.

| service | pattern | payload | writer | reader | history | buffer | safe overflow | max writers | max readers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `<base>/command` | pub/sub | `Command` | client | daemon | 0 | 4 | true | 4 | 1 |
| `<base>/command` | event | — | client | daemon | — | — | — | 4 | 1 |
| `<base>/command_status` | pub/sub | `CommandStatus` | daemon | client | 1 | 256 | true | 1 | 8 |

Up to four clients may hold a command publisher; the lease token, not the
port, decides whose commands are written. `command` has no history (a daemon
that starts never acts on a command sent before it) and keeps the newest
four. `command_status` keeps 256, one per command the daemon took.

## 3. Messages

The conventions are those of `arm_feedback.shm/1` §3. The type names carry
this contract's major (`omakase_arm_command_v1_*`).

### 3.1 Layouts

#### `Header` — `omakase_ee_v1_Header`, 32 bytes, align 8

| offset | size | field | type |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | 4 | `schema_version` | `u32` |
| 4 | 4 | `reserved` | `u32` |
| 8 | 8 | `instance_id` | `u64` |
| 16 | 8 | `seq` | `u64` |
| 24 | 8 | `mono_ns` | `u64` |

#### `Command` — `omakase_arm_command_v1_Command`, 168 bytes, align 8

| offset | size | field | type |

| 52 | 4 | `flags` | `u32` |

#### `CommandStatus` — `omakase_arm_command_v1_CommandStatus`, 360 bytes, align 8

| offset | size | field | type |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | 32 | `header` | `Header` |
| 32 | 8 | `client_instance_id` | `u64` |
| 40 | 8 | `client_seq` | `u64` |
| 48 | 8 | `client_mono_ns` | `u64` |
| 56 | 8 | `rx_mono_ns` | `u64` |

| 72 | 8 | `accepted` | `u64` |

| 232 | 128 | `message` | `u8[128]` |

### 3.2 Codes and bits

| name | value | meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `SIDE_MASK_A` | 1 | `Command.sides` bit: arm A (index 0, the physical left arm) |
| `SIDE_MASK_B` | 2 | `Command.sides` bit: arm B (index 1) |
| `RESULT_ACCEPTED` | 0 | `CommandStatus.result`: written to the arm controller |
| `RESULT_INVALID` | 1 | another schema version, no sender, no side or an undefined one, a flag set, a non-finite target |
| `RESULT_STALE` | 2 | older than its age limit when the daemon took it |
| `RESULT_LEASE` | 3 | the token is not the live lease's: no lease is held, it expired, or it is another holder's |
| `RESULT_SOFT_KILLED` | 4 | the arm soft-kill latch stands |
| `RESULT_NOT_READY` | 5 | an addressed arm is not in a mode that acts on joint targets, or the controller's firmware family contradicts the protocol |
| `RESULT_GUARD` | 6 | the motion guard refused the path (a clearance) |
| `RESULT_BUSY` | 7 | another arm operation holds an arm (a recover, a lease hand-over) |
| `RESULT_RATE` | 8 | above the path's rate ceiling |
| `RESULT_DEVICE` | 9 | the write to the arm controller failed |
| `RESULT_LIMITS` | 10 | a target outside the joint limits |
| `CMD_SLOWED` | 1 | `CommandStatus.flags` bit: the approach limiter wrote a shorter step than the target |

### 3.3 What each message means

**`Command`** (client): `target_deg` holds arm A's and arm B's seven joint
targets in degrees, the unit of `POST /v1/arm/move_joints_both`; an arm
`sides` does not name is not read. `lease_token` is the `token` of the
answer to `POST /v1/arm/lease` (16 hexadecimal digits, read as a `u64`): a
renewal keeps it, a fresh grant — after the lease expired, was released or
was preempted — draws another. `max_age_ns` is how old the command may be
when the daemon takes it, by its header `mono_ns`: `0` for 50 ms, at most
1 s.

**`CommandStatus`** (daemon), one per command taken: which command
(`client_instance_id`, `client_seq`, `client_mono_ns`), when the daemon took
it (`rx_mono_ns`) and when its write to the controller returned
(`sent_mono_ns`, `0` when refused), the result, the written targets
(`written_deg`: the sent ones, or the approach limiter's shorter step with
`CMD_SLOWED` and the per-arm `scale`), the refusal's message, and the path's
cumulative counters. `sent_mono_ns - rx_mono_ns` is the daemon-added
latency; `sent_mono_ns - client_mono_ns` adds the transport and the wait for
the daemon's thread.

## 4. Rules a client MUST follow

1. Take the arm lease (`POST /v1/arm/lease`, or `arm.lease.acquire` over
   the WebSocket frontend) before streaming, renew it well inside its
   `ttl_s`, and carry its token in every command.
2. Publish each command with a fresh header (`seq` strictly increasing from
   1, `mono_ns` when the command was produced), then notify the `command`
   event service.
3. Publish no faster than the ceiling `arm_status.shm/1` reports
   (`max_command_hz`); the excess is refused.
4. Read `command_status` for its own `client_instance_id`, at least as often
   as it publishes 256 commands, and treat a refusal as the arms holding the
   last accepted target — not as a stop it has to undo: the next command
   that passes every check is written.
5. On `RESULT_LEASE`, stop streaming until it holds the lease again; the
   lease was lost (expired, released, preempted) and the token with it.
6. Put the arms in a mode that acts on joint targets (`POST
   /v1/arm/{side}/mode`) itself; this contract does not change modes.

## 5. What the daemon does

**One thread.** An OS thread of its own, outside the daemon's async
runtime, owns the services. It sleeps on the `command` event service, or at
most 10 ms, so a client that does not notify is still served within 10 ms.
When it wakes it takes every command waiting, keeps the newest and counts
the others as superseded (no status is published for them).

**The checks, in order:** the contract's (§3.3: header, sides, flags,
finite targets, age) → the rate ceiling (`[arm] shm_max_command_hz`, at most
1000 per second; a bucket holding 20 ms of commands) → the lease token
against the live lease → the daemon's facade, exactly as for `POST
/v1/arm/move_joints_both` (both arms; a streamed tick through the approach
limiter and the path guard) or `POST /v1/arm/{side}/move_joints` (one arm;
the path guard): joint limits, soft-kill latch, arm exclusion, mode, guard.
The write is one datagram to the arm controller.

**A refusal stops the stream and locks nothing.** The first refusal after an
accepted command publishes `EVENT_STREAM_STOPPED` (with the refusal's
`RESULT_*` and message) on `arm_events.shm/1` and sets
`STATUS_STREAM_STOPPED` on `arm_status.shm/1`; the arms hold the last
accepted target. The first accepted command after it publishes
`EVENT_STREAM_RESUMED`. Nothing has to be unlocked: a lost lease is taken
again with a new token, and a guard refusal clears as soon as a command
leaves the guard's margins (or moves out of them).

**The budget.** The daemon-added latency (`sent_mono_ns - rx_mono_ns`) is
budgeted at p99 ≤ 0.5 ms and max ≤ 2 ms at 200 Hz. Measured in the
simulator on a workstation (release build, `d1-arm-loopbench`): at 200 Hz
p50 90 µs, p99 443 µs, max 470 µs; at 1 kHz p50 69 µs, p99 174 µs, max
360 µs; every command written. The robot's figure is measured on the robot
with `d1-arm-loopbench`.

## 6. Versioning

- Any change to a layout, a code, a bit or a service setting is a new major
  version: `arm_command.shm/2`, type names `omakase_arm_command_v2_*`, a
  `2.layout.json` sibling, and `schema_version` 2.
- Version 1 is frozen. The SHA-256 of `1.layout.json` is
  `987222e48bb079782e01a5d541b0bf19017a67fdf53dbacbc4172d278493c4be`; the crate pins it
  (`layout::COMMAND_LAYOUT_SHA256`), and the daemon pins it again in its own
  tests.

## 7. Conformance checklist for a client

1. Runs as the daemon's account; builds its node with the root and prefix
   of `arm_feedback.shm/1` §1; iceoryx2 0.9.3.
2. Opens `<base>/command` (publisher), the `<base>/command` event service
   (notifier) and `<base>/command_status` (subscriber) with the settings of
   §2.
3. Defines `Command` and `CommandStatus` with exactly the layout of
   `1.layout.json` and the type names it lists, and checks the file's
   SHA-256 in a test.
4. Follows the rules of §4.

## 8. A worked example

`d1-arm-loopbench` on a robot, as the daemon's account, both arms in
position mode:

```bash
d1-arm-loopbench --rate-hz 200 --duration-s 600 --sides ab \
  --amplitude-deg 1.0 --period-s 4.0 \
  --min-rate-hz 199 --max-p99-us 500 --max-max-us 2000 \
  --max-frame-miss 19 --max-refused 0 --json loopbench-200hz.json
```

1. It reads one `arm_feedback.shm/1` record and keeps both arms' measured
   joints as the centre.
2. It takes the lease over REST as `d1-arm-loopbench` (class `operator`) and
   reads the token from the answer; a thread renews the lease every 10 s.
3. Every 5 ms it publishes a `Command` (both arms, every joint at the
   centre plus a 1° sine of 4 s period, its token, the default age limit),
   notifies, and takes the waiting `command_status` and feedback samples.
4. After 600 s it releases the lease and prints the written rate, the
   daemon-added latency p50/p99/max, the end-to-end latency to the first
   feedback record whose `accepted_cmd` is the written target, the highest
   `frame_miss_count` per arm and every refusal; a gate not met exits 1.
